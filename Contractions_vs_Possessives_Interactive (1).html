Squish or Own?

Contractions vs. Possessives · Grade 7 Grammar Practice

Squish

Contraction

Two words join into one. The apostrophe (’) replaces the missing letters.

do not → don’t

they are → they’re

The “o” in not is missing. The “a” in are is missing.

Own

Possessive

An apostrophe + s shows who owns something.

Maria’s backpack

the girls’ coats

One owner: ’s. Many owners that already end in -s: s’.

Tricky Twins

Squish (contraction)Own (possessive)
it’s = it isits = belongs to it
they’re = they aretheir = belongs to them (there = a place)
you’re = you areyour = belongs to you
who’s = who iswhose = belongs to who

Possessive pronouns never use an apostrophe: his, hers, its, ours, yours, theirs.

Watch Out: Plurals

A plural just means more than one. It does not get an apostrophe.

The dogs ran. (plural)  ·  The dog’s bone. (possessive)  ·  The dog’s tired. (contraction: dog is)

Tip: say each sentence out loud. If you can say it as two words, it’s a contraction.
